Your Proxmox VE servers and clusters in Swarm. Its page has an overview of the cluster: nodes online, guests running, CPU and memory, what needs attention (a node offline, a guest locked, a storage almost full), the busiest guests and the last tasks. Browse every VM and container by node, filtered and searched by name, vmid or tag, the cluster's tasks with their logs, each storage and how full it is, and each node with its usage, graphs, versions and syslog.

Open a VM or container to start, shut down, reboot, pause or stop it, open its console in Proxmox, see its CPU, memory, network and disk graphs over the last hour, day or week, its IP addresses, its hardware and notes, and its tasks. Take snapshots, with a VM's memory if you like, roll back to one or delete one. Back up now to any storage or Proxmox Backup Server, restore a backup as a new guest or over the old one, and see which guests were never backed up or not this week. Clone a guest or make a new one from a template, move it to another node, and change its name, cores, memory, tags and whether it starts at boot. Whatever takes a guest down asks first, and each action follows its task to the end and tells you how it went.

Open a container's console, a node's shell or a VM's serial console right in Swarm, beside the guest. Each node's disks show their wear and SMART health, and a failing disk shows up in Needs attention. Connect as many servers as you like and switch between them in the sidebar; every server's backups are watched, and a failed one is a toast even when the page is closed.

Agents get swarm proxmox (servers, status, list, show, start, shutdown, reboot, stop, suspend, resume, snapshots, snapshot, rollback, delete-snapshot, backups, backup, clone, migrate, set, nodes, storage, tasks, task-log) and a skill that has them ask you before they take a guest down or roll it back. The token stays in your keychain: they never see it.

Connect each server in Settings, Proxmox, with its address and an API token from Datacenter, Permissions, API Tokens, or a user and password, with your authenticator's code if you use two-factor sign-in. Proxmox's own self-signed certificate is shown by its fingerprint, to compare with the one in Proxmox, and trusted once you say so: that certificate alone. It works with Proxmox VE 7 and 8, on your own network, over Tailscale or behind a proxy.
